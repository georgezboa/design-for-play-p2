## 1. Narrative spine

The transition films are not travel montages. Together they form one continuous change in Butch:

1. He begins by asking the train to help him find Mara.
2. He learns that Mara is alive and deliberately staying ahead of him.
3. He learns that she left by choice, which removes his right to treat pursuit as rescue.
4. He continues while knowing that patterns can be accurate and still become the answer he wants.
5. He reaches an archive that tries to erase those distinctions and preserve only one explanation.

The central dramatic question is:

> Can Butch care about Mara without converting her choices into evidence that she belongs with him?

The Archivist is the opposing logic. It does not need every fact to be false. It needs every fact to be forced into one clean answer.

## 2. Required grammar for every transition

Every film must perform three jobs in this order:

1. **Consequence:** begin with a physical result of the chapter the player just completed.
2. **Carry:** preserve one object, mark, or relationship state across the boundary.
3. **Question:** end on a conflict that the next chapter will test through play.

A change in art style, location, weather, or train exterior is not enough by itself.

### Recurring objects

- **Mara's photograph:** Butch's original reason for travelling. It is a person reduced to an image, so framing and cropping it anticipates the Museum.
- **The cyan thread:** a real connection deliberately left by Mara. It can lead, slacken, knot, or pass through objects, but it never drags Butch against his will.
- **The punched Night Service ticket:** the train's accepted route. Each chapter may add a physical mark, fold, stain, burn, or accession number to the same ticket.
- **Butch's hands:** initially repair and follow; later reconnect, assemble, and finally resist classification.

### Color and material meaning

- Cyan means a connection Mara deliberately preserved.
- Amber means a consequence moving from one action into another system.
- Red means coercion, system fault, or a forced answer.
- White crop frames mean the Archivist removing context.
- Black is held only at authored chapter boundaries. It is not filler between unrelated shots.

### Dialogue rules

- Dialogue is short, dry, and specific.
- Do not narrate what the image already communicates.
- Mara is heard only through a letter or previously authored message until the story explicitly permits more.
- The Archivist speaks only when a system is classifying, rejecting, or compressing evidence.
- Exact English subtitles are composited after picture lock. Never ask a video generator to render readable text.

## 3. CIN 01 to 02: THE TRAIN ACCEPTS THE SEARCH

Narrative job: turn the completed Night Service repair into Butch's first deliberate pursuit of Mara. Establish that the train can follow contact with its line, not reveal a person's destination or intention.

Target duration: 27 seconds.

Carry object: punched Night Service ticket, Mara's photograph, cyan thread.

Next chapter question: can a route-finding system locate a person who chose not to leave a destination?

### T12-01: The last consequence | 0:00 to 0:05

Begin on the real final state of Chapter 1. The last machine settles. Butch releases the ticket press only after the train's relays answer in sequence. The completed punched ticket slides into his hand. One fresh hole holds restrained amber light.

Do not open on a generic exterior train shot. The player's final action owns the first frame.

Sound: punch release, pressure settling, five relays answering from near to far.

### T12-02: A person is not a destination | 0:05 to 0:10

Butch places Mara's photograph beside the punched ticket on the worn timetable surface. The train's brass route fingers search the photograph, find no printed destination, and stop. A thin cyan fibre is caught under one corner of the photograph.

CARETAKER, filtered:

> Route restored. Destination absent.

BUTCH:

> Then take me to the last place she touched the line.

The train must not identify Mara, promise rescue, or claim to know why she left.

### T12-03: The train answers literally | 0:10 to 0:15

Amber light leaves the punched ticket and travels through the visible door relay, brake linkage, roof conduit, and pantograph. Each device responds only after the pulse physically reaches it. The cyan fibre stays separate from the amber current and passes through the window seal.

Sound: carriage rhythm tightens into an electrical pulse.

### T12-04: Current becomes city | 0:15 to 0:22

Follow the amber pulse outside. The ordinary mountain railway enters a tunnel. Inside the tunnel, the roof conduit becomes a suspended copper grid. The walls fall away into the inhabited vertical city of Borrowed Grid. Amber wakes lifts, clinic power, water pumps, and evacuation lights, not decorative signs.

The cyan fibre is visible on a distant maintenance ladder, tied deliberately rather than snagged by accident.

### T12-05: Butch chooses the first step | 0:22 to 0:27

The train door opens onto the real Chapter 2 start composition. Butch pockets the ticket and photograph, tests the cyan fibre with two fingers, then releases it. He steps out under his own power.

CARETAKER, filtered:

> Contact ends ahead.

BUTCH:

> Then I start there.

Hold the exact next-chapter plate for the final 45 frames. No generated interface text and no duplicate chapter title.

## 4. CIN 02 to 03: THE LETTER BECOMES EVIDENCE

Narrative job: begin with the Mara letter the player was required to read at the final balcony. Confirm that she passed through Borrowed Grid and continued voluntarily. Transform a private message into the first public trail in Echo City.

Target duration: 28 seconds.

Carry object: Mara's letter, cyan stitch, punched ticket.

Next chapter question: was Mara escaping danger, escaping Butch, or doing something that neither explanation captures?

### T23-01: The required letter | 0:00 to 0:06

Begin on the real final balcony. Butch removes Mara's folded letter from beneath the door hardware. Its cyan stitch is wet from the city rain. The completed parkour route remains visible behind him, including one moved ladder and a departing patrol car.

MARA, letter voice:

> Butch, I made it through this city, but I could not wait here.

This line is current Chapter 2 canon and must not be paraphrased.

### T23-02: She stayed ahead by choice | 0:06 to 0:11

Butch reads the second fold. The rooftop mechanic remains distant and does not interrupt the letter. On the page, a simple hand-drawn eastbound arrow ends beside a small clock mark. The mark is composited cleanly in edit.

MARA, letter voice:

> The train opened the next door before dawn. I went on.

The added arrow and clock are route notation, not proof of motive.

### T23-03: The answer is incomplete | 0:11 to 0:16

Inside the departing train, Butch places the letter over Mara's photograph. The two identify the same person but answer different questions. He folds both around the punched ticket rather than discarding either.

MARA, letter voice:

> If you are following me, keep moving. I will leave another mark where I can.

Butch does not answer aloud.

### T23-04: Grid becomes chronology | 0:16 to 0:23

Outside the window, three cyan electrical nodes blink in sequence. Match cut to three civic lamps surrounding Echo City's clock. Copper grid lines become street-map rules; rain becomes long afternoon shadow; the neon palette drains into stone, oxidized brass, and muted watercolor.

The letter's cyan stitch becomes a blue-pencil circle around one departure time, then disappears under the fold before the time can be read as a magical instruction.

Sound: relay sequence becomes clock ticks, then a tram bell one beat late.

### T23-05: A private search enters public record | 0:23 to 0:28

The train arrives on the real Echo City platform. Butch steps down alone holding the photograph, with the folded letter and ticket in his inner pocket. The conductor remains in the carriage. Lev is not pre-introduced in the film.

CONDUCTOR:

> Echo City. Keep the doorway clear.

End on the exact Chapter 3 opening plate so the existing arrival dialogue can begin without repeating exposition.

## 5. CIN 03 to 04: DIRECTION IS NOT PERMISSION

Narrative job: preserve the complete Chapter 3 conclusion. Mara is alive, left by choice, built the route alone, and travelled east. Butch chooses to continue without converting those facts into permission. Transform Echo City's evidence discipline into Chapter 4's three archives and shared MOON inference.

Target duration: 29 seconds.

Carry object: original reservation, punched ticket, cyan thread, three-column evidence fold.

Next chapter question: can Butch recognize a true pattern without turning it into the explanation he wants?

### T34-01: What the fire proved | 0:00 to 0:06

Open on the real morning remains of the two ground lines. The fire is gone. The words survive as blackened channels and loose ash. Butch's thumbprint remains beside the reconnected clamp.

GROUND LETTERS, remembered, not newly spoken:

> BUTCH, I'M ALIVE.

> I LEFT BY CHOICE.

The morning evidence, not a dream image, owns the frame.

### T34-02: Three facts, one missing reason | 0:06 to 0:12

On the platform, Lev places Mara's original eastbound reservation beside Butch's folded letter and the burned route rubbing. Butch sorts them into three simple columns on one sheet: known, unsupported, still testable. Exact labels are composited in edit.

BUTCH:

> Alive. By choice. Eastbound.

LEV:

> That gives you a direction. It does not give you her permission.

This line is the emotional hinge of the full route.

### T34-03: Butch boards alone | 0:12 to 0:17

The eastbound train arrives. Butch folds the three-column sheet around the reservation, letter, and ticket. Lev remains on the platform. The cyan thread passes from inside the fold toward the carriage, but it stays slack until Butch chooses to board.

BUTCH:

> I know.

He boards alone. Do not show Mara.

### T34-04: Evidence becomes paper law | 0:17 to 0:24

Inside the tunnel, the three evidence columns become three paper archive panels. The reservation's blue pencil becomes the cyan connection line. The burned groove rubbing becomes graphite construction. The amber consequence line passes through the panels but does not merge them.

Each archive briefly shows a different historical image. All three contain the same small moon sign, but nothing claims that they share one explanation.

Sound: rail clatter becomes pencil pressure, page turns, and three soft pigment taps.

### T34-05: Read before interpreting | 0:24 to 0:29

The paper panels fold outward into the real Chapter 4 opening space. The first Color Link archive is visible but not yet solved. The incomplete train is glimpsed farther ahead as a future consequence, not placed beside the player early.

BUTCH, quiet:

> Start with what repeats.

End on the exact current Chapter 4 first frame. Do not return to the superseded cold stove, Mara mural, blank scar, or old wash-and-bridge Bay A handoff.

## 6. CIN 04 to 05: THE ARCHIVE REMOVES THE COST

Narrative job: begin with the actual Chapter 4 consequence. Butch recognized MOON across three records, earned six pigments, assembled the train, and escaped while residents chased him because the route cost them material and color. The Museum then tries to preserve the successful answer while removing the people who paid for it.

Target duration: 30 seconds.

Carry object: three archive folios, six-pigment HUE ring, MOON ignition mark, train-wheel study, residents' testimony.

Next chapter question: who controls the meaning of a journey, the people who lived it or the system that catalogues it?

### T45-01: The correct answer moves the train | 0:00 to 0:06

Begin on the real assembled Chapter 4 train. Butch presses MOON as the ignition decision. The six HUE pigments travel through the train from wheels upward, confirming the same bottom-up support order the player used to build it.

The engine starts. This is success, not triumph.

Sound: six pigment tones resolve into one low rail chord.

### T45-02: The cost follows | 0:06 to 0:12

Nine residents run close behind the departing train. Their drained objects remain graphite outlines in the background. Keep the existing small speech bubbles and crowd rhythm. Do not replace them with an abstract threat.

RESIDENT VOICES, overlapping and incomplete:

> You took the color.

> That was our bridge.

> Bring it back.

Butch looks back. The train continues because he chose to leave.

### T45-03: Success is edited | 0:12 to 0:18

Inside the moving carriage, the three archive folios, six HUE pigments, MOON ignition mark, and train-wheel study lie on the floor. White crop frames descend. They preserve the solved pattern and completed train, but crop out the residents at the window and the drained objects outside.

ARCHIVIST:

> Successful reconstruction. Contradictory cost removed.

Red alignment marks appear only where the crop forces unlike evidence into one row.

### T45-04: Butch keeps the contradiction | 0:18 to 0:24

Butch places his hand across the closing crop. The punched ticket, Mara's letter, Echo City reservation, and Chapter 4 folios become four distinct evidence cases instead of one clean answer. The cyan thread crosses all four without turning them into one story.

BUTCH:

> A route is not a reason.

ARCHIVIST:

> One answer can still be preserved.

BUTCH:

> Then keep what does not agree.

### T45-05: The journey becomes an exhibit | 0:24 to 0:30

Paper folds become vellum labels. Pigment becomes accession color. Train windows become glass cases. The moving carriage slows into the real Museum corridor while the residents' final footsteps remain faintly audible under the HVAC.

The four chapter dossiers are already visible:

1. punched ticket, cyan thread, relay;
2. rooftop hardware and Mara's letter;
3. oil-route map, work order, reservation;
4. archive folios, pigment ring, train-wheel study.

End on the exact current Chapter 5 first frame with Butch entering from the authored doorway. No first-person white hands on the rails. No generic museum title generated inside the image.

## 8. Editorial assembly rules

- Use motivated match cuts. Ticket hole to relay, cyan stitch to map line, burned groove to graphite, wheel pigment to accession color.
- Keep Butch's face at medium distance. Identity continuity matters more than facial acting.
- Do not show Mara's body or face in these four films.
- Preserve authored black at true chapter boundaries, but eliminate dead black used as missing content.
- Dialogue should sit above environmental sound rather than replacing it. Duck music 4 to 6 dB during lines.
- Keep each subtitle to two lines, 42 characters per line where possible, and at least 72 px above the 1200 px master bottom edge.
- Do not place subtitles over interactive prompts in an exact gameplay handoff plate.
- Final 45 frames of every film must be a clean next-chapter gameplay plate. Any ambient motion should come from the game after control returns.
- Current 1280 x 720 footage must be reframed or regenerated for the 8:5 production master. Do not crop Butch's head, the carried object, or the next-chapter destination to force aspect ratio.

## 9. Canon exclusions

Do not reintroduce any of the following:

- a visible second Mara travelling with Butch;
- a promise that Mara needs rescue;
- an Archivist that attacks through generic glitches or explosions;
- the superseded Chapter 4 cold-stove opening as the current handoff;
- the superseded Mara home mural or player-chosen blank scar;
- a Chapter 4 ending without the three archive readings, six HUE pigments, MOON ignition, completed train, and crowd chase;
- a Museum containing unrelated surreal objects instead of chapter evidence;
- any line that treats `I LEFT BY CHOICE` as proof that Mara remained safe afterward;
- any line that treats eastbound evidence as permission for Butch to continue.

## 10. Acceptance gates

1. Each film opens on a consequence the player personally caused.
2. Each film carries one physical object from the previous chapter into the next.
3. A first-time viewer can state what Butch learned after each transition.
4. A first-time viewer can state what remains unknown after each transition.
5. Chapter 2 to 3 includes Mara's required final-balcony letter.
6. Chapter 3 to 4 preserves alive, left by choice, eastbound, unknown reason, and Butch boarding alone.
7. Chapter 4 to 5 preserves MOON, six pigments, train assembly, resident cost, and the four Museum dossiers.
8. Cyan, amber, red, white crop frames, and black retain their shared meanings.
9. Butch does not confuse direction with permission in dialogue or framing.
10. The Archivist's threat is forced interpretation, not physical destruction alone.
11. No generated readable text, pseudo-interface copy, or duplicate chapter title survives the final edit.
12. The final 45 frames match the next chapter's current gameplay plate closely enough that the handoff reads as one continuous shot.

## 11. Recommended production order

1. Produce the Chapter 2 to 3 letter insert first. It uses current canon, replaces existing near-black footage, and tests the new narrative grammar at the lowest cost.
2. Produce the Chapter 3 to 4 platform and evidence opening. It locks the emotional hinge before more media is generated.
3. Rebuild Chapter 4 to 5 around MOON, the crowd, and archival cropping. This is the most important and most extensive replacement.
4. Finish Chapter 1 to 2 after the exact Chapter 1 final gameplay plate is captured, so ticket press continuity and the first handoff can be matched precisely.
5. Composite exact subtitles, sound transitions, and final gameplay plates only after all picture edits are locked.
