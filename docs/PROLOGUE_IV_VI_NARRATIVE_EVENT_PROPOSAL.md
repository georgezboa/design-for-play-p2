# PROLOGUE IV–VI — THE TRAIN CARRIES WHAT HAPPENED

**Status:** PROPOSED / REQUIRES I–III CONTINUITY CORRECTION  
**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Scope:** Replace the rejected Phase IV balance rig and the existing Phase V–VI

**2026-08-06 correction:** this proposal's archive event is retained, but IV–VI

## 1. Player promise

> A stalled archive train begins to wake, its stored memories fall loose, and
> the player must help the train carry contradictory lives without throwing one
> away. By the end, the train remembers how the player acted and answers back.

This is not a maintenance exam. The mechanical systems are stage machinery for
the relationship between Butch, the train, the absent passengers and Mara.

## 2. Narrative turn

Phases I–III prove that Butch can make the train respond physically. Phase IV is
the first attempt to depart. That attempt wakes an Archivist purge routine:
stored passenger records are classified as excess, contradictory weight and
released from the ceiling archive rack.

The apparent problem is overload. The real problem is the Archivist's rule that
only one version may remain. The train cannot speak, but it resists the purge by
opening hidden supports and replaying Butch's earlier actions. Butch's conduct
becomes the evidence that two conflicting records can be carried together.

The sequence advances three story facts:

1. the train contains human lives, not abstract data;
2. the Archivist mistakes contradiction for corruption;
3. the train learns from what Butch does and becomes a partner.

## 3. Form

- One fixed theatrical cutaway, not three unrelated rooms and not a full-screen
  puzzle overlay.
- Point-and-click / click-and-drag blocking: Butch walks automatically to large,
  visible objects; the player is staging a crisis, not performing platforming.
- Upper passenger salon and overhead archive rack remain visible while the
  suspension and wheelset react below. No HUD meter is required.
- Three acts reuse the same physical set under new lighting, motion and meaning.
- A mistake causes a slow, readable physical consequence—tilt, sliding luggage,
  a support folding, an echo fading—and recovers in place. No red failure card.

## 4. Act IV — THE FALLING ARCHIVE

### Story beat

Butch asks the train to depart. The starter turns once, the lamps dim, and three
archive cases drop from the ceiling rack onto a suspended baggage cradle. The
carriage leans; tableware, hanging lamps and Butch all react to the same tilt.
Each case briefly projects a human action in a window: tying a scarf, carrying a
battery, painting a stone. These are future-world foreshadows, not exposition.

### Player action

The player drags the large cases along the overhead rail. Their mass visibly
tilts a single equalizing beam and compresses the corresponding suspension.
There is no numerical balance target. The readable goal is to keep both wheel
pairs planted while all three cases sit inside brass witness brackets.

### Development

1. One case falls near center: drag it into either bracket; the train steadies.
2. A second, heavier case falls: the first arrangement no longer works; the
   player redistributes both.
3. A third case begins to fall outside the supported span. The existing cradle
   cannot hold everything. The scene pauses on that impossibility and flows
   directly into Act V.

The first two beats teach a legible physical sentence: **what the train carries
changes how the train stands**. The third beat turns that sentence into story.

## 5. Act V — TWO TRUE THINGS

### Story beat

The Archivist outlines two incompatible cases in sterile white frames and opens
a floor-level jettison door. A calm line states: `ONE RECORD MUST BE REMOVED.`
Either case can be thrown away and the train will become mechanically level—but
the corresponding human echo disappears. This is a temptation, not the answer.

The train answers with two quiet knocks. An unused second cradle unfolds beneath
the first, but it has no support. This is the only moment where the earlier
mechanical vocabulary returns:

- the cyan pneumatic branch can inflate the second cradle's cushion;
- the brass electrical lead can power its small winch;
- the cases provide the weight that proves whether the new support is real.

These are three large spatial relationships on one machine, not five labelled
buttons. Connecting air visibly lifts the cradle. Connecting power visibly winds
the cable. Placing a case tests the structure.

### Player problem

Create capacity for both contradictory cases, then distribute them across the
two cradles. There are multiple stable arrangements. The insight is not a hidden
sequence; it is **do not choose which memory deserves to exist—build another way
to carry it**.

### Failure and recovery

- Power without air: the winch pulls, the unsupported cradle folds and releases.
- Air without power: the cradle floats but drifts out of position.
- Excess weight on one cradle: the whole set leans and the cases slide slowly
  back to their last witness brackets.

Every failure shows the missing relationship and keeps all discovered state.

## 6. Act VI — THE TRAIN REMEMBERS

### Story beat

The train begins moving. The windows finally scroll, ceiling dust falls backward
and the archive set becomes a live carriage. An amber echo of Butch repeats the
case movement recorded in Act IV on the first cradle. A new case begins sliding
on the second cradle in the present.

### Player action

The player mirrors the echo's weight with the present case. This is a duet in
space rather than a timing prompt: when the past crosses left, the present must
counter it on the right. The beam, lamps, suspension and window horizon make the
relationship readable continuously.

### Twist

The Archivist redacts part of the recorded echo. A saved case starts to fall.
The obvious optimal action is to remain at the balance position; the human action
is to leave it and catch the case. When Butch does so, the train performs the
missing counter-movement by itself, reusing the pattern it learned from the
player. The train is no longer an object being repaired. It has become a partner.

### Payoff

Butch catches the case, the train catches Butch, and both supports lock. Amber
pulses run through the actions Butch actually performed rather than through a
prewritten checklist. The train departs. In the window reflection, Mara's cyan
thread is tied around one of the saved witness brackets and her silhouette is
visible for one beat.

No new control is introduced in this final act.

## 7. Why this replaces the rejected design

- IV–VI are one event with an emotional escalation, not three maintenance
  minigames.
- Weight is both physical material and narrative meaning.
- Earlier air/electrical vocabulary returns once, to express making room for two
  truths—not as a recap exam.
- The past echo changes the player's role and the train's character; it is not a
  decorative timer.
- The player can explain success in story language: `I helped the train keep both
  memories, then it remembered how I helped.`

## 8. Explicit exclusions

- No full-screen control panel, five-button strip, pressure percentage or green
  target band.
- No launched bearing, receiver, A/B bogie checklist, generic TEST sequence or
  red warning banner.
- No requirement to know railway engineering vocabulary.
- No text that gives the physical solution.
- No unchanged interaction repeated more than twice.
- No implementation before target-frame selection.

## 9. Target-frame gate

After George approves the premise, create exactly three visual targets before
coding:

1. **IV / first fall:** wide theatrical cutaway, one archive case impacting the

2. **V / forced choice:** two contradictory cases held in white Archivist frames,

3. **VI / duet:** moving night landscape, amber past Butch and present Butch on

The selected targets define composition, object scale, light hierarchy and the
minimum asset list. They are not pasted runtime backgrounds.

## 10. First-time-player acceptance

A first-time player, without spoken help, must be able to say:

1. `The cases are making the train lean.`
2. `The system wants me to throw one memory away, but the train showed me another
   support.`
3. `My past movement and my present movement are balancing each other.`
4. `The train copied what I did so it could save me.`

If the player instead describes valves, buttons or an unexplained success state,
the narrative design has failed even if the state machine passes every test.
