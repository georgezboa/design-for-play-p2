# NIGHTFALL — Story bible (canon v1, 2026-09-28)

Decided by Claude on George's delegation ("你来决定"). Everything here is deliberately collected in one file so George can veto or edit a line and the chapters can follow. Where a chapter contradicts this file, this file wins.

## The premise
- **The night service** is "the last archive line": a train that runs between stored places. It is not time travel. It is an archive of places and of people's records.
- **The Conductor** keeps the line running. The line only runs while someone is riding it.
- **Butch** is the night service's lost-property clerk. He is **not related to Mara**. He's a clerk who could not leave an unclaimed case alone.

## Mara Velez (real, 1978)
- In 1978 she kept a rented **city room** near the terminal, for the early shift, and still went home to the **orchard house in Bellwether** at weekends.
- Her younger sister is **Rosa Velez**, who lived at the orchard.
- The archive filed her two lives as **two claims** (claim 1978-0412). The **city case** was collected. The **orchard case** stayed on the train.
- The line from the file that the whole game rests on: *"There is no missing-person report in this file. The property was lost, not Mara."*

## The Mara ahead (the echo)
- Every chapter shows a figure of Mara one step ahead (a silhouette, a ticket stub, a letter "keep moving", a reservation). That figure is **the Conductor's echo**: a record of Mara made to walk, so that Butch keeps riding and the line keeps running.
- The player shouldn't be told this before the true ending. It is planted fairly:
  - her face is never seen;
  - her letters are always slightly too well-timed;
  - the archive's second claim keeps spawning a second "Mara".
- **"M. Venn"** (Chapter 3) is the archive's corrupted duplicate of the second claim: the terminal cuts long surnames on duplicate tickets, so VELEZ became VENN. Chapter 3's two-tickets-43 mystery is that duplicate. Chapter 5's line "The archive does not issue duplicates" is the museum lying.

## Chapters (player-visible names)
1. **NIGHT SERVICE**: returning the orchard case; "two true things".
2. **BORROWED LIGHT**: the city runs on time borrowed from the train; Mara's letter "keep moving".
3. **ECHO CITY**: the duplicate ticket; M. Venn; move as one; the scanner.
4. **THE PAINTED COUNTRY**: Rosa's childhood drawings of the orchard, painted over by the archive. Mara's mark is the **hawthorn**.
   - The Chapter 4 symbol set is: answer **HAWTHORN**; large marks **TICKET**, **LANTERN**, **APPLE**; decoy **ROSE** (a wrong answer triggers a line about Rosa).
   - The train there is the **PAINTED TRAIN**, not "Borrowed Train".
5. **THE MUSEUM OF ONE ANSWER**: the archive has already filed Butch's journey and wants one clean answer about Mara. Its accession record is "ACC. 1978-0412 · VELEZ, M. · PENDING".
6. **ALL WORLDS AT ONCE**: the Conductor.

## Magic stones
- There are five, one per chapter plus one in the Museum: EMBER (Ch1), GRID (Ch2), ECHO (Ch3), PIGMENT (Ch4, rename the label "COLOR STONE" to PIGMENT), and **BLACK TICKET** (Museum).
- The last one's player-visible name changes from "Black Knife". The code ids stay (`black-knife`).
- They are records the archive could not file. Holding all five lets Butch reach the line's last carriage.

## Endings
- **Normal ending** (0–4 stones): Butch outlasts the Conductor. The train keeps running with Butch still aboard, still looking one stop ahead.
  - Add one short archive card before the credits: "CLAIM 1978-0412 · STATUS: OPEN · The orchard case remains on board."
- **True ending, "The Unfiled Ending"** (5 stones): the hidden fight is against **the Black Ticket**, the Conductor's true form: the ticket that never gets punched, the line that never ends.
  - After it, the reveal: the Mara ahead was an echo the Conductor filed to keep Butch riding. The real Mara lived both her lives in 1978.
  - Butch gets off at **Bellwether** and leaves the orchard case at Rosa's door.
  - Final lines: *"The property was lost, not Mara."* / *"CLAIM 1978-0412 · CLOSED."*
- Remove every "Butch never had a sister" line.

## Tone
Melancholy and patient, with warm lamps in the rain. Light meta-humour is fine inside the fiction (archive forms, stamps). Do not parody real websites. Keep archive cards to 2–3 short lines.
