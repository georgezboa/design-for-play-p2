## 1. Where this chapter sits in the full game

Butch woke on the night train without a stable name. The train remembers what
people do, not what they are called. Each world seed gives him another partial,
contradictory account of Mara and of himself.

At the end of Chapter 2, Butch restores power to an evacuation tower. A woman
called Mara, wearing the recurring cyan scarf, boards the night train with the
other passengers.

The next carriage door opens onto an orderly civic square. Through the scanner
field, Butch sees another woman with the same face and the same cyan scarf. She
is sitting beside the fountain under a public notice:

> `TRANSIT STATUS SUSPENDED — DUPLICATE PASSENGER RECORD`

This is the first time the contradiction becomes a living person rather than a
reflection or an archive hint. The chapter does not explain which Mara is real.
It asks whether Butch can help this person without forcing her to answer that
question for him.

## 2. The dramatic engine

### Butch's urge

**He needs the second Mara to recognize him.**

Butch has no dependable biography. If this woman knows him, she can tell him
whether he saved her, abandoned her, or was ever part of her life. He is not
entering the square from abstract curiosity. He is frightened that the woman on
the train may be the wrong Mara, and more frightened that neither of them will
know him.

### Immediate objective

**Reach the woman at the fountain and bring her onto the night train before the
18:00 civic movement lockdown.**

The objective is stated in ordinary language after the opening camera reveal:

> `REACH THE WOMAN AT THE FOUNTAIN.`

After Butch reaches her, it changes to:

> `GET BOTH OF YOU BACK TO THE TRAIN.`

### External obstacle

The city's movement system has canceled the woman's transit pass because an
active passenger with the same identity already exists on Butch's train. The
scanner does not merely read papers or faces. It flags movement that does not
fit an accepted public pattern. Butch cannot cross alone, and the second Mara
cannot be ordered to follow him.

### Internal obstacle

Butch treats Mara as evidence about himself. His instinct is to reach her first,
ask his questions, and walk ahead while she follows. That is exactly what the
final scanner rejects.

### Character need

**Stop leading. Pay attention to the person beside him.**

The final action is not selecting the correct argument. Butch and Mara must
match pace, spacing and direction through the last scanner. The relationship
sentence remains the full-game lock:

> **Safety can come from coordination rather than control.**

## 3. Concrete narrative question

The chapter's question is:

> **Can Butch bring a second Mara onto a train that already contains one?**

The player begins by asking, “Which Mara is real?” The chapter ends by making a
different question more urgent: “Will Butch make room for both before he knows?”

Chapter 5 still owns the full revelation that the archives disagree about
whether Butch saved, abandoned or never met Mara. Chapter 3 only makes the
contradiction physical and personal.

## 4. Chapter flow

```text
NIGHT TRAIN / see second Mara at fountain
  → attempt direct crossing and get returned by scanner
  → investigate market movement and the canceled transit pass
  → speak with Eda and Clerk Sava
  → learn one accepted carry–wait–return public routine
  → use that routine to reach Mara
  → lockdown alarm disperses the crowd
  → Mara speaks; Butch learns she can see the other Mara on the train
  → walk beside Mara and build a two-person pattern
  → cross the final scanner together
  → the train accepts both passengers
```

The route has one optional loop through the Archive steps. It adds context and
reopens one failed conversation approach, but it is never required.

## 5. Beat structure and pacing

| Time | Beat | Main action | Narrative change |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:30 | **The woman in the square** | Step off the train, inspect the platform and see Mara at the fountain. | Butch and player share one concrete goal. |
| 1:30–3:00 | **Direct attempt** | Walk toward her; scanner returns Butch to the clock-side curb. | Papers and intention are not enough. Movement is the gate. |
| 3:00–8:00 | **Market routine** | Touch and inspect small objects, talk to Eda, watch the porter and learn carry–wait–return. | The city rewards recognizable public behaviour. |
| 8:00–11:00 | **Duplicate passenger** | Use queue objects and talk to Clerk Sava about Mara's canceled pass. | The other Mara on the train is the reason this Mara cannot move. |
| 11:00–13:00 | **Borrowed crossing** | Perform the porter routine through the scanner and reach the fountain. | Butch can imitate belonging but still treats Mara as his answer. |
| 13:00–14:30 | **Lockdown** | Crowd disperses; first substantial Mara dialogue. | The borrowed crowd pattern is gone. Mara refuses to be led. |
| 14:30–18:00 | **Walk beside me** | Match Mara's pace through two scanners and return to the tram. | A two-person relationship becomes a valid pattern. |
| 18:00–20:00 | **The second passenger** | Train door test and short closing exchange. | The train accepts contradiction without resolving identity. |

## 6. Dialogue style rules

The language target is literary realism with sharp internal observation. It is
not a collection of riddles or generalized philosophy.

1. People speak first about their job, inconvenience, money, shift, paperwork,
   fear or immediate obligation.
2. Every internal observation begins from a concrete visible detail: torn paper,
   bruised fruit, shoe wear, cold metal, repeated ink pressure.
3. A line may become funny or emotionally charged, but it cannot float free of
   the object or person that caused it.
4. NPCs do not explain the setting. They answer only what they would reasonably
   know and often defend the part of the system they depend on.
5. Butch's response choices reveal attitude. They do not present three different
   ways to request the same exposition.
6. Maximum normal line length is two short sentences. Long monologues are not
   used during ordinary play.
7. Internal voices may be confident and wrong. They are interpretations, not
   tutorial narrators.

## 7. Internal voices

Use three compact faculties. They speak only when an object or exchange gives
them something specific to notice.

| Faculty | Concern | Typical mistake |
|---|---|---|
| **PATTERN** | Systems, timing, sequence, physical evidence. | Believes a complete mechanism is the same as the truth. |
| **TENDERNESS** | Fear, embarrassment, attachment, what people avoid saying. | Turns uncertain people into a comforting story. |
| **NERVE** | Authority, immediate danger, shame turned into action. | Mistakes force for honesty. |

There is no character-creation screen. Choices during the first three object
interactions raise the voice the player repeatedly trusts. Later options reflect
that history without showing a large RPG stat sheet.

## 8. Exact opening dialogue

The camera starts on the tram threshold. A public-address speaker crackles while
the second Mara is visible beyond the clock.

**PUBLIC ADDRESS**  
`Civic movement review begins at eighteen hundred. Travelers without an active
pass must remain where they are.`

**BUTCH**  
`Mara?`

The woman looks toward the train. She does not stand.

**PATTERN**  
`Same scarf. Same height. Different coat. She is looking at the second carriage
window, not at you.`

**TENDERNESS**  
`She heard the name. Her shoulders moved before her face did.`

**NERVE**  
`Twenty metres of paving and one camera. Go.`

Player responses:

1. `Walk straight to her.`
2. `Look at the woman inside the train first.`
3. `Inspect the public notice.`

All remain valid. A direct walk safely demonstrates the scanner return. Looking
inside the train shows only the first Mara's silhouette in the window, keeping
the contradiction visible without a cinematic explanation.

## 9. Eda, the produce seller

Eda is closing her stall slowly because lockdown cancels street trade but does
not refund the day's permit. She knows the fountain woman as a regular customer,
not as a cosmic mystery.

### First exchange

**EDA**  
`If you're buying fruit, buy it. If you're asking about the woman, take a queue
number like everybody else.`

Responses:

1. `What queue?`
2. `Why is she sitting there?`
3. `Two apples.`
4. `[NERVE] You know I am not here for fruit.`

**EDA, if asked why**  
`Her pass stopped working before lunch. The Ministry told her to wait. She has
been very obedient about it, which is probably why nobody has helped.`

**EDA, if buying apples**  
`She buys two every Thursday. One sweet, one cheap. Pays exact.`

**TENDERNESS**  
`A habit small enough to survive official attention. Eda trusts it more than the
name on the pass.`

**PATTERN**  
`Two apples. Two fixed positions on the scale. The porter waits for the needle,
then turns. The scanner sees the whole exchange as one repeated unit.`

### Gameplay output

- Eda points to the brass queue dispenser at the Transit Ministry.
- Buying/weighing the apples causes the porter to perform the complete
  carry–wait–return routine in a safe area.
- The player may observe the routine directly or reconstruct it by touching its
  three physical anchors: handcart, scale and receipt spike.

## 10. Clerk Sava, Transit Ministry

Sava has thirteen minutes left in the shift and no authority to resolve a
duplicate-person record. He is defensive because he knows the rule is harming
someone and has already decided that helplessness is the same as innocence.

### Opening

**SAVA**  
`Number forty-three.`

The dispenser gives Butch forty-three. Mara's abandoned ticket on the counter is
also forty-three.

**PATTERN**  
`Same number. The machine did not advance after her. The queue has been waiting
for a person the clerk already refused.`

Responses:

1. `Her pass says DUPLICATE PASSENGER.`
2. `There is another woman with her face on my train.`
3. `Restore the pass.`
4. `[TENDERNESS] What happens to her at eighteen hundred?`

**SAVA, duplicate passenger**  
`The pass is not expired. Expired records stay in the system. This one was
canceled because another active passenger answered to the same identity.`

**BUTCH**  
`The other passenger is on my train.`

**SAVA**  
`Then your train is the problem registered to this case.`

**NERVE**  
`There is always a person inside the passive voice. He is sitting in front of
you with ink on his thumb.`

**SAVA, asked about lockdown**  
`The scanner returns her to the fountain until a collection vehicle arrives.`

**BUTCH**  
`A police vehicle?`

**SAVA**  
`The notice does not use that word.`

### Fail-forward approaches

- Threatening Sava makes him shut the window, but he leaves the temporary
  movement form under the glass where Butch can take it.
- Treating him gently makes him stamp the form himself and admit that paper only
  tells the scanner where a person ought to be.
- Presenting the train's punched witness ticket makes him visibly afraid and
  reveals that the Archivist ordered duplicate cancellation that morning.

Every approach gives the same critical information. Tone and later lines change;
progress does not depend on winning a check.

## 11. Reaching Mara

Butch uses the learned carry–wait–return routine through the first scanner. The
routine is performed semantically by movement, not through rhythm-button input.

At the fountain:

**BUTCH**  
`Mara. The train is behind me. We need to go now.`

She looks past him toward the train and remains seated.

Player responses:

1. `Do you know me?`
2. `There is another you on the train.`
3. `Your pass was canceled because of us.`
4. `Stand up. We have four minutes.`

Before she answers, lockdown begins. Stalls close, the porter leaves, the public
groups disperse and the borrowed behaviour loses its safe classification.

**PUBLIC ADDRESS**  
`Civic movement review is now active. Unscheduled groups must separate.`

Mara stands.

**MARA**  
`You brought her with you.`

**BUTCH**  
`You can see her?`

**MARA**  
`Second window. She has my scarf tied around her wrist.`

Responses:

1. `Which one of you is Mara?`
2. `Does she know me?`
3. `I came back for you.`
4. `We can argue on the train.`

**MARA, to “which one”**  
`The clerk asked me that for an hour. It did not improve with repetition.`

**MARA, to “does she know me”**  
`You reached me and asked about yourself.`

**MARA, to “I came back”**  
`You came to collect an answer. I am still deciding whether to come with it.`

These lines state Butch's flaw without turning Mara into a lecturer. She is
angry about what he is doing now, not explaining the game's theme.

## 12. Final movement and closing dialogue

Butch clicks toward the train. If he moves ahead at ordinary solo speed, the
scanner marks the pair as separating and returns them to the fountain-side
cover.

After the first failure:

**MARA**  
`Stop walking ahead of me.`

**BUTCH**  
`The scanner is closing.`

**MARA**  
`I know. Match me.`

The final crossing uses the existing three visible synchronized footsteps,
spacing correction and paired lane change. No new controls appear.

At the tram door, the train checks both punched tickets. The second Mara's pass
remains visually canceled. The train opens anyway and produces the amber witness
pulse learned in Chapter 1.

**MARA**  
`When the door opens, do not introduce us.`

Responses:

1. `I won't.`
2. `I need to know which of you remembers me.`
3. `She already knows you are here.`
4. `Walk in beside me.`

**MARA, final shared line**  
`Then stay beside me.`

Butch and Mara cross the threshold together. Through the carriage window, the
two cyan scarves occupy the frame at the same time. The chapter ends before
either woman is forced to explain the other.

## 13. Object interaction grammar

The square should feel dense because objects have jobs, wear, ownership and
small physical reactions. Clicking an object must do at least one of four things:

- teach the movement system;
- advance the duplicate-pass problem;
- reveal ordinary life under the city's rules;
- expose Butch's tendency to turn evidence into identity.

No object exists only to produce a disconnected poetic sentence.

Use three interaction types:

- **INSPECT:** close observation and one short internal response;
- **HANDLE:** a small physical action or state change;
- **SOCIAL:** an object changes or opens a conversation.

## 18. First-play acceptance

Without designer explanation, a player must be able to say:

1. `There was already a Mara on my train, so the city canceled this Mara's pass.`
2. `I copied a public routine to reach her.`
3. `She would not follow me; I had to walk at her pace.`
4. `The train accepted both people even though the city would not.`

The player should inspect at least five optional objects because they are
visually inviting or react physically, not because a checklist asks for them.

If the player describes the story as a missing-time mystery, a symbolic dream,
or an abstract argument about identity, the writing has failed. The people,
paperwork, objects and immediate deadline must remain legible first.
